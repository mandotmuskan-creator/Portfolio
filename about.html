About

Oh, hello.

I’m Muskan — a designer who likes turning messy ideas into clear interfaces. Six years in, still mostly interested in the boring questions people skip.

I design digital products across web and mobile. Most of my work starts a long way from a screen — with who is on the other side, what they are trying to do, and which steps in the way of it do not earn their place.

Then I build the interface, and the system that keeps it consistent after I’ve gone. Tokens, components, states, annotations. The part nobody screenshots, and the part that decides whether a redesign is still standing a year later.

Right now I’m at Dentsu Global Services in Pune, working across enterprise web, loyalty products and the design systems that hold them together.

Ask me about the sensor catalogue. I have opinions.

Process

The way I work

Nothing here starts with a screen. It starts with a question about who is going to use the thing.

  1. 01

    Ask the boring questions first

    How often does this person sign in? What do they already know? The unglamorous answers are where the interesting decisions come from.

  2. 02

    Map it before you draw it

    Journeys, flows and states on paper first. It is much cheaper to move a step than to redesign a screen.

  3. 03

    Clarity over decoration

    Hierarchy, then contrast, then everything else. If someone has to hunt for the one thing that matters, the layout has not earned its keep.

  4. 04

    Leave a system behind

    Components, variants, tokens and annotations — built to be handed over, not just admired.
