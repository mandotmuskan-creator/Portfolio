UX & UI Designer

Hello,

I design digital products for web and mobile — and the part I care about most happens before anything gets drawn. Who is on the other side of this screen? How often do they come back? What do they already know, and what are we making them learn for no good reason?

That means the work usually starts in FigJam rather than Figma: auditing what exists, mapping journeys and flows, writing the interface copy, and arguing about which steps have actually earned their place. Then I design the interface, take it through iterations with stakeholders and engineers, and leave behind a documented system so it stays consistent after I have moved on.

I have worked across sensors and hardware, retail and loyalty, fintech, enterprise SAP tooling and health — plus presales and client-facing work, which is really just the same skill pointed at a room full of opinions.

Outside of it all I like new places, unfamiliar food, and noticing how badly signage is designed in airports.

The work, stage by stage

Not just the pretty end

Understand

Auditing what already exists, reading whatever research is on the shelf, and talking to the people who answer support tickets. Most of the real problems are already known by someone — they have just never been written down in one place.

Four things I keep coming back to

Ask the boring questions

How often does this person sign in? What do they already know? The most interesting design decisions come out of the least interesting answers.

Clarity first

Hierarchy before decoration. If someone has to hunt for the one thing that matters, the layout has not earned its keep yet.

Systems, not screens

Components, variants and tokens, documented with their states. The difference between a redesign that lasts and one that drifts by spring.

Warmth counts

Even enterprise software is used by someone having a day. A little softness in the colour, the copy and the spacing goes a long way.

the fine print —

I am, admittedly,
a bit of a klutz.

I have knocked a full coffee onto a printed moodboard the morning of a review. I have tripped over the one cable in an otherwise empty room. I once sent a file named final-FINAL-v3-actually.fig and meant it sincerely.

None of that ever made it into the work — if anything it is why I am so stubborn about the systems. When you know you will drop something eventually, you build the kind of structure that survives being dropped.
